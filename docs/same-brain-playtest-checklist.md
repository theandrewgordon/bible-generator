# Same Brain playtest checklist

Use this with 3–6 households before calling the next content pass complete. Ask
players to use their own phones and avoid explaining what a question is “supposed”
to mean.

## Core paths

- [ ] Everyone: finish Daily Brain and Fresh 5.
- [ ] Kids: a child completes five questions without adult translation.
- [ ] Tween/Teen: a 10–15-year-old completes five questions without calling them childish.
- [ ] Mixed Ages: one child and one adult answer the same five together.
- [ ] Start Together: create on one device, join on another, finish in either order,
  close one browser, return, and confirm the result recovers.
- [ ] Challenge: send a completed challenge, answer it on another device, and
  confirm the creator sees the response.
- [ ] Group Brain: create a five-question group, join with three people, finish
  in different orders, and confirm the result updates.

## Record after every game

For each question, record:

- exact question ID or a screenshot;
- whether every answer choice made sense;
- whether the player had a clear answer within five seconds;
- whether the question felt age-appropriate;
- whether it felt too similar to a previous question;
- whether the wording sounded natural when read aloud.

## Exit criteria

- No question is reported as nonsensical by two or more players.
- No child needs an adult explanation for Kids or Mixed Ages questions.
- No immediate repeat appears within a player’s recent history.
- Start Together and Group recover after a refresh or temporary disconnect.
- At least 80% of players finish a five-question game.
- At least 3 of 5 invited challenge recipients open and complete a challenge.

Keep raw notes separate from analytics identifiers. Do not collect children’s
names, ages, photos, or free-form personal information in the playtest sheet.
