# Full GUI Audit — 2026-10-11

## How this was checked
- **Pages:** all 70 GET pages on `main` (`fdb5f0b`), in a real browser (Chromium).
- **Login states:** each page twice, signed out and signed in as an admin.
- **Layouts:** desktop at 1366×900, plus phone at 390×844 with touch in both light and dark mode. That's 420 page loads and 502 screenshots.
- **Checks per page:** JavaScript errors, failed requests, broken images, sideways scrolling on phones, how many `<h1>` headings and `<title>` tags it has, small tap targets, and an automated **axe-core WCAG 2 AA** accessibility scan (light mode).

**The big picture:** the app is in good shape. There are no JavaScript errors and no broken images. On phones nothing scrolls sideways except three admin pages. The problems are mostly a few **shared-style bugs that show up on every page**, plus **dark mode being applied unevenly**.

Not counted as bugs, because they come from running locally:
- Pages that need Firestore or Stripe (`/billing`, `/prints`, `/admin/users`, `/admin/collections`, and WeekFlow's "couldn't open" states) show a fallback.
- Signed-out visits to sign-in pages redirect to Google.

## P1 — visible to most visitors

### 1. "Sign in" is invisible in the header [verified]
`_navbar.html` renders `<a class="nav-link">Sign in</a>` inside `.auth-theme`, but `theme.css` only styles `.nav-links a`. So the link inherits dark body text (#1f2937) on the dark green header (#19332d). The contrast is 1.08:1, so it's effectively invisible.
- **Who sees it:** every signed-out visitor on desktop. That's the main way to sign in.
- **Fix:** style `.auth-theme .nav-link` like the main nav links: header text color, button padding, a visible border.

### 2. The phone menu button is broken and the logo sits off to the right [verified]
- `.menu-toggle .bar { display:inline-block }` lays the three bars **side by side**, so the menu icon looks like one long dash ("———"), not ☰.
- On phones the logo also sits next to the button instead of on the left.
- **Fix:** `.bar { display:block }`, and pin the logo to column 1 on the left (`.logo-nav > .logo { justify-self:start }`).

### 3. Brand teal fails contrast on buttons and small labels [verified, axe: 48 of 70 pages]
- **White text on #0ea5a8 is 3.01:1.** Normal-size text needs 4.5:1. This covers primary buttons: "Play a free game", Plus "Apply", Downloads "Unlock bundle", "Open Plan Worship" and others.
- **Teal small labels on light backgrounds** ("CORE" badges, kickers, `#0ea5a8` on `#edf2f7`) are 2.67:1.
- **Fix: one token change.** A darker button teal of **#0b8184** gives 4.68:1 with white. For small text on light panels, **#0a7a7d** gives 4.56:1. Keep the bright teal for large shapes and borders.
- **Close but not passing:**
  - Speed Die primary button #397bb5: 4.49:1.
  - Games-page blue #157ad1: 4.39:1.
  - Gray helper text #6b7280 on #edf2f7: 4.29:1.
  - All three need a shade darker.

### 4. Dark mode is broken in places [verified]
- **`/family-game-night`:** the hero headline and paragraph stay dark navy on the dark background and are nearly unreadable.
- **`/labs/games` Games Player panel:** the panel stays cream but its labels turn white, so "Player", "+ New Player", "Sound Effects" and "Music" disappear.
- **Neon accent:** dark mode turns teal buttons a bright green (`Start with your family`, `Play a free game`) next to teal ones. It looks accidental.
- **Pages that ignore dark mode entirely:** the games sub-apps (Family Bible Bee, Group/Church Games, Family Game Night play, Speed Die, Same Brain) and WeekFlow stay light. That's fine, but it should be deliberate.
- **Fix:** either opt those pages out of the global dark theme explicitly (`data-theme="light"` on their roots), or give them their own dark colors. Fix the two broken spots above directly.

### 5. Links with no style fall back to browser blue and purple [verified]
`theme.css` has no global `a {}` rule. Any link without its own class renders in default #0000EE, or purple once visited. Examples:
- "Start here" on the home page.
- "← Back to Worship" and "How to import a song" on `/worship/add`.
- The song titles and "Back to Worship" on `/worship/licensing`.
- The "Back to Worship" link on `/worship/presentation/import`.
- The support email address.

**Fix:** one global rule: `a { color: var(--primary-dark); text-underline-offset: 2px }` plus a dark-mode color.

## P2 — polish and consistency

6. **12 pages share the tab title "Faith Sparks":** every Worship page plus `/copyright`. With several tabs open you can't tell Worship tabs apart. Set titles such as "Plan Worship · Faith Sparks" and "Add Song · Faith Sparks".

7. **Three or four visual systems.**
   - The main site uses a dark green header and the Inter font.
   - The Games pages (Family Game Night, Bible Bee, Group Games) have a cream background, a serif "FaithSparks" logo and *no main header*.
   - WeekFlow has its own green header.
   - Same Brain is purple and Speed Die is blue.

   **Headlines also switch fonts:** the home page and About use a sans-serif, while Families, Churches, Prepare, Play, Labs and the Games pages use Georgia serif. The family brand would feel steadier if:
   - every page used the same headline font, and
   - every standalone game page had a small consistent "← Faith Sparks" link back to the main site.

8. **Desktop width and alignment vary.** Some pages are centered (Verse of the Week, Worship help index). Others are a narrow left-hand column with the right ~55% of the screen empty: `/about`, `/copyright`, `/terms`, `/privacy`, `/worship/deck/history`, the Worship getting-started guides. Center the reading pages in one container width.

9. **The home page hero on phones is long.** You scroll past three stat chips, the proverb card and the game card before reaching "What would help today?". On desktop the chips ("FOR REAL FAMILY LIFE / Mixed-age friendly") wrap awkwardly. Make the chips one compact row and move the proverb and game below the task cards.

10. **The theme toggle is a wide empty pill** (about 150 px wide holding only 🌙). A square icon button would read as a button.

11. **The footer** is tiny text with bullets running across lines on phones. Bump the size to 13–14 px and stack the links in two columns on small screens.

12. **`/worship/licensing` on phones:** the table collapses into a column of stray "0" and "—" values. Show each song as a card with labeled fields.

13. **WeekFlow `/labs/weekflow/settings`** shows a **blank page** when its data fails to load. The other WeekFlow pages show a "couldn't open · Try again" card. The "couldn't open" cards also come in two layouts: large centered serif, and small left-aligned.

14. **WeekFlow landing on phones:** the four top links ("← Faith Sparks Labs", "Open WeekFlow Today (Home)", …) wrap into a ragged two-line block. Use one back link plus a primary button.

15. **Several `<h1>`s on most WeekFlow pages** (2–3 each). Use one per page and make the others `<h2>`.

16. **Accessibility gaps the automated scan found** (critical):
    - `/worship/add`: the photo upload, YouTube start time, "Slide type" and "Photo layout" fields have no label.
    - `/admin/theme`: the preview-minutes and logo upload fields have no label.
    - `/labs/weekflow`: the demo progress area scrolls but can't be reached by keyboard.

17. **Admin pages scroll sideways on phones:** `/admin/theme` (146 px), `/admin/content` (52 px), `/admin/theme/new` (27 px). This is low priority since admin is desktop work.

## Noted, not GUI
- **Meta Pixel and children's games.** The Meta Pixel loads on every page except `/admin`, including the kids' games, Bible Bee and Same Brain. For signed-in users it also sends a SHA-256 hash of their email ("advanced matching"). Check that this matches your privacy policy and your audience, especially on children's pages. Turning the pixel off on game and Labs routes would be the conservative choice.

## What's working well
- No JavaScript errors or broken images on any page, in any state.
- Phone layouts are clean: no sideways scrolling outside admin, readable type, large tap targets on the core flows.
- The Worship builder, getting-started guides and Same Brain read as polished, purposeful screens.

3. The two broken dark-mode spots (#4), and opt the games pages out of the global dark theme.
4. Page titles (#6) and the labels the scan flagged (#16).
5. Polish in any order: #8–#15.
